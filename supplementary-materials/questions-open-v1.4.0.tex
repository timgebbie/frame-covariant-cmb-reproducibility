\paragraph{Q16 is the cheaper of the two and it is one line.} Assert
$\mathcal V(\eta_{\rm couple})/\mathcal V(\eta_*)$ below a stated threshold on
\emph{every} background and raise with the number if not --- \textbf{P7}, both
backgrounds, every time. If $6$ is comfortable everywhere the assertion costs
nothing and documents why; if it is marginal on one, that is a finding nobody
currently holds. \textbf{It cannot move a peak either way}: what is frozen is
$\delta T$ and $\tau_1$, which after decoupling have no scattering to drive
them, and the line-of-sight integral itself runs to $\eta_0$ on a grid whose
density is gated against the Bessel kernel's own period (finding \textbf{T-3}).

\paragraph{A finding, not a question, that belongs beside these.}
\textbf{T-67} is open and is load-bearing for the low-$\ell$ plateau rather
than a loose end. \texttt{coefficients.py} builds $(a\mathcal B_1)'$ with two
further terms behind \texttt{A\_B1\_PRIME\_EXPANSION\_TERMS} ---
$-a\mathcal H\,\delta T-3a\mathcal H\,\Phi_A$ after dividing through by $k$ ---
and whether they belong is unsettled. They are $O(a\mathcal H)$, and the late
integrated Sachs--Wolfe effect lives where $k\sim a\mathcal H$, so the reduced
form is not available in exactly the regime the plateau occupies.
\textbf{Any statement of $(a\mathcal B_1)'$ that omits them is the
expansion-terms-off form and should say so.}
